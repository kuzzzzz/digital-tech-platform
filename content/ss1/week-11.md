# WEEKS 11–13 — REVISION AND EXAMINATIONS

## WEEK 11 — REVISION

### Objectives
To consolidate all topics taught in the term, address weaknesses identified in the midterm test, and drill examination technique.

### Suggested Two-Period Plan

**Period 1 — Rapid topic sweep (45 minutes)**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 10 min | Weeks 1 & 2 — definitions, analogue vs digital, generations of computers |
| 15 min | Week 3 — number system conversion drill at the board (this is where marks are won or lost) |
| 10 min | Week 4 — hardware: block diagram, input/output classification, RAM vs ROM |
| 10 min | Week 6 — software classification chart reproduced from memory |

**Period 2 — Examination technique and past questions (45 minutes)**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 10 min | Week 8 — OS functions and booting; Week 9 — laboratory rules and safety |
| 10 min | Week 10 — one image and one sound file-size calculation worked on the board |
| 20 min | Timed practice: 20 objective questions in 15 minutes, then marked together |
| 5 min | Examination instructions, materials required, and the timetable |

### Examination Technique to Teach Explicitly
1. Read the whole paper before writing; note how many questions must be answered from each section.
2. Allocate time by marks — a 15-mark question deserves roughly three times the time of a 5-mark question.
3. Answer the questions you know best first to build confidence and secure marks.
4. In "state" and "list" questions, give exactly the number asked for and number your points.
5. In "explain" and "discuss" questions, write full sentences — a bare list loses marks.
6. In "differentiate" questions, use a **table with two columns** and make each pair of points compare the *same* feature.
7. In calculations, **show all working** and state the unit at every stage; marks are awarded for method even when the final answer is wrong.
8. Draw diagrams with a pencil and ruler, label every part, and give the diagram a title.
9. Never leave an objective question blank.
10. Leave five minutes to check the paper, especially unit conversions and question numbering.

### SAMPLE THEORY QUESTIONS — SS 1 DIGITAL TECHNOLOGIES, FIRST TERM
*Answer any FOUR questions. Each carries 10 marks.*

**1.** (a) Define digital technology and state four of its characteristics. **(6 marks)**
 (b) State four ways digital technology has transformed banking in Nigeria. **(4 marks)**

**2.** (a) Draw a table showing the five generations of computers with the technology used and one example of each. **(5 marks)**
 (b) State the contribution of any five pioneers to the development of the computer. **(5 marks)**

**3.** (a) Convert 231₁₀ to (i) binary (ii) octal (iii) hexadecimal. **(6 marks)**
 (b) Convert 10111011₂ to hexadecimal using the grouping method. **(2 marks)**
 (c) Add 1101₂ and 1011₂. **(2 marks)**

**4.** (a) Draw and label the block diagram of a computer system. **(5 marks)**
 (b) State five differences between RAM and ROM. **(5 marks)**

**5.** (a) Draw the classification chart of computer software. **(4 marks)**
 (b) Differentiate between a compiler and an interpreter. **(3 marks)**
 (c) State three functions of an operating system. **(3 marks)**

**6.** (a) State six computer laboratory rules. **(3 marks)**
 (b) Explain three health hazards of prolonged computer use and how each may be prevented. **(3 marks)**
 (c) An image measures 640 × 480 pixels with a colour depth of 24 bits. Calculate its file size in megabytes. **(4 marks)**

### MARKING GUIDE — SELECTED ANSWERS
- **Q3(a):** 231₁₀ = **11100111₂** = **347₈** = **E7₁₆**
- **Q3(b):** `1011 1011` → **BB₁₆**
- **Q3(c):** 1101₂ + 1011₂ = **11000₂** (13 + 11 = 24 ✔)
- **Q6(c):** 640 × 480 × 24 = 7,372,800 bits ÷ 8 = 921,600 bytes ÷ 1,048,576 = **0.88 MB** (≈900 KB)

## TEACHER'S HELPER NOTES — WEEKS 11–13
- **Revision should be active, not a re-lecture.** Students who watch you re-explain a topic learn far less than students who attempt a question and then hear the correction. Aim for at least half of Week 11 to be students working.
- **Target revision using midterm data.** You already know from Week 5 which topic the class failed. Give that topic double the revision time, whatever the balance looks like on paper.
- **Number systems and data representation calculations should dominate revision** — they carry the most marks and are the least forgiving of vague answers.
- **Give the sample paper as a mock**, mark it, and go through it. Students meeting the format for the first time in the hall lose marks to unfamiliarity rather than ignorance.
- **Insist on tabular answers for "differentiate" questions.** It is the single easiest technique improvement, and Nigerian examiners reward it.
- **Diagram practice:** the block diagram of a computer and the software classification chart appear constantly. Have every student draw both from memory before the examination.
- **After the examination, do a topic-by-topic error analysis** before you write your term report. It tells you exactly where to strengthen the Second Term, and it is the difference between teaching the scheme and teaching the class.
