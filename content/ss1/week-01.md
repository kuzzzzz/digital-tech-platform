# WEEK 1 — INTRODUCTION TO DIGITAL TECHNOLOGIES

**Sub-topics:** Meaning of digital technology · Analogue vs digital · Scope and branches · Relevance in modern Nigerian society · Benefits, challenges and careers

## Behavioural Objectives
By the end of the lesson, students should be able to:
1. define digital technology in their own words;
2. distinguish between analogue and digital systems with at least three examples of each;
3. list and briefly describe at least six branches of digital technology;
4. state and explain at least six areas where digital technology is applied in Nigeria today;
5. discuss four benefits and four challenges of digital technology in Nigeria;
6. name at least five careers in the digital technology industry.

## Entry Behaviour
Students already use mobile phones, ATMs, POS terminals, social media and CBT examinations. They have offered Basic Science and Digital Technologies at the JSS level.

## Instructional Materials
Chalkboard/whiteboard, a mobile phone, an analogue wall clock and a digital clock or phone clock, a laptop or desktop computer, a flash drive, printed pictures of a POS terminal, ATM and BVAS machine, projector and charts where available.

## PERIOD 1 (45 minutes)

### 1.1 Meaning of Digital Technology
**Digital technology** refers to electronic tools, systems, devices and resources that generate, store, process and transmit data in the form of **discrete values** — usually the two digits **0 and 1** — rather than as continuously varying signals.

The word *digital* comes from *digit*, meaning a numeral. Everything a digital device handles — a photograph, a voice note, a text message, a bank balance — is ultimately reduced to a long string of 0s and 1s called **binary code**.

A simpler classroom definition: *Digital technology is any technology that works by converting information into numbers (0s and 1s) so that machines can store, process and share it.*

### 1.2 Analogue vs Digital
**Analogue technology** represents information as a **continuous** physical quantity — a wave, a voltage that varies smoothly, the position of a pointer.
**Digital technology** represents information as **separate, countable steps**.

| Feature | Analogue | Digital |
|---|---|---|
| Signal type | Continuous wave | Discrete steps (0s and 1s) |
| Accuracy | Affected by noise and distortion | High and stable |
| Copying | Quality drops with each copy | Copies are identical to the original |
| Storage | Bulky (tape, vinyl, film) | Compact (flash drive, cloud) |
| Editing | Difficult | Easy and reversible |
| Examples | Wall clock with hands, mercury thermometer, vinyl record, analogue radio, film camera, landline telephone | Digital clock, digital thermometer, MP3 file, DAB/online radio, digital camera, smartphone |

> **Important distinction:** *electronic* is not the same as *digital*. An old analogue radio is electronic but not digital. All digital devices are electronic, but not all electronic devices are digital.

**Nigerian illustration — the Digital Switch-Over (DSO):** Nigeria has been migrating television broadcasting from analogue transmission to **Digital Terrestrial Television**. The same amount of spectrum now carries far more channels, with clearer pictures and extra services such as electronic programme guides. That is the practical difference between analogue and digital in one example.

### 1.3 Characteristics of Digital Technology
1. **Binary representation** — all data is coded in 0s and 1s.
2. **Programmability** — the same device can perform different tasks when given different instructions.
3. **Accuracy and precision** — results are exact and repeatable.
4. **Lossless duplication** — a copy is identical to the original.
5. **Compressibility** — data can be squeezed into smaller sizes for storage and transmission.
6. **Networkability** — devices can be connected and can share data over distances.
7. **Speed** — millions of operations per second.
8. **Convergence** — one device (the smartphone) now performs the work of a camera, radio, torch, calculator, map, bank and television.

### 1.4 Scope and Branches of Digital Technology

| Branch | What it deals with | Everyday example |
|---|---|---|
| Computer hardware | Physical devices and their design | Laptops, phones, servers |
| Software engineering | Writing and maintaining programs | Building a school portal |
| Networking & internet | Connecting devices to share resources | Wi-Fi, fibre, mobile data |
| Data science & Artificial Intelligence | Extracting meaning from data; intelligent systems | Fraud detection, chatbots |
| Cybersecurity | Protecting systems and data from attack | Antivirus, firewalls, encryption |
| Digital media & graphics | Images, audio, video, animation, design | Nollywood editing, Canva designs |
| Robotics & embedded systems | Machines that sense and act | Drones, smart meters |
| Cloud computing | Computing power and storage delivered over the internet | Google Drive, iCloud |
| Financial technology (fintech) | Digital financial services | Opay, Moniepoint, Paystack |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling online | Jumia, Konga, Jiji |

## PERIOD 2 (45 minutes)

### 1.5 Relevance of Digital Technology in Modern Nigerian Society

**a. Education** — JAMB conducts the UTME entirely as a **Computer-Based Test**; WAEC and NECO results are checked online with scratch cards or e-PINs; schools run e-learning platforms, and universities use portals for registration and results.

**b. Banking and finance** — the **Bank Verification Number (BVN)** links a customer's biometrics to all their accounts; ATMs, POS agents, USSD codes such as \*737#, and mobile apps such as Opay, PalmPay, Moniepoint and Kuda have brought banking to people who never entered a banking hall. Transfers are cleared through NIBSS.

**c. Governance and identity** — the **National Identification Number (NIN)** issued by NIMC; INEC's **BVAS** for voter accreditation and the **IReV** portal for result viewing; the Treasury Single Account and Remita for government revenue; online tax filing with FIRS.

**d. Health** — electronic medical records, telemedicine consultations, digital diagnostic equipment, SMS reminders for immunisation.

**e. Agriculture** — weather and market-price apps for farmers, e-wallet systems for input distribution, drones for crop monitoring.

**f. Commerce and business** — online marketplaces, digital advertising, inventory software, WhatsApp Business.

**g. Transportation** — ride-hailing (Bolt, Uber, inDrive), GPS navigation, e-ticketing for rail and air travel.

**h. Entertainment and media** — Afrobeats distributed worldwide through streaming platforms; Nollywood films on Netflix, Prime Video and Showmax; online radio and podcasts.

**i. Security** — CCTV, emergency call centres, digital forensics.

### 1.6 Benefits
1. **Speed** — tasks that took days are completed in seconds.
2. **Accuracy** — machines do not get tired or careless.
3. **Massive storage** — a single flash drive holds what once filled a room of files.
4. **Improved communication** — instant contact across the world at low cost.
5. **Financial inclusion** — millions of unbanked Nigerians now transact digitally.
6. **Job creation** — software development, digital marketing, data analysis, freelancing for foreign clients.
7. **Transparency and accountability** — digital records are traceable and auditable.
8. **Access to knowledge** — free courses, journals and tutorials online.

### 1.7 Challenges in the Nigerian Context
1. **Unreliable electricity** — devices cannot run without stable power; generators and inverters add cost.
2. **Cost of devices and data** — laptops and monthly data remain expensive relative to income.
3. **Digital divide** — rural communities have weaker network coverage and fewer facilities than urban ones.
4. **Cybercrime** — internet fraud, phishing, identity theft and hacking.
5. **Inadequate infrastructure and skilled manpower** — many schools lack functional computer laboratories and trained teachers.
6. **Electronic waste (e-waste)** — discarded devices pollute the environment and contain toxic metals.
7. **Job displacement** — automation removes some routine jobs.
8. **Misinformation and social harm** — fake news, addiction, cyberbullying.
9. **Brain drain** — trained technology professionals emigrating ("japa").

### 1.8 Careers in Digital Technology
Software developer/programmer · Data analyst/data scientist · Cybersecurity analyst · Network/systems engineer · Database administrator · UI/UX designer · Graphics and multimedia designer · IT support technician · Computer hardware engineer · Digital marketer · Cloud engineer · Artificial intelligence engineer · Computer science teacher/lecturer · Technical writer.

**Subjects to pursue these careers:** Mathematics, English, Physics, Digital Technologies/Data Processing, and Further Mathematics or Economics depending on the pathway.

## EVALUATION — WEEK 1

### A. Objective Questions (1 mark each)
1. Digital technology represents data mainly in the form of ______.
 (a) waves (b) 0s and 1s (c) letters (d) colours
2. Which of these is an analogue device?
 (a) Smartphone (b) Mercury thermometer (c) Flash drive (d) Laptop
3. The BVN in Nigerian banking stands for ______.
 (a) Bank Value Number (b) Bank Verification Number (c) Basic Verified Name (d) Banking Virtual Node
4. Which is **not** a branch of digital technology?
 (a) Cybersecurity (b) Cloud computing (c) Photosynthesis (d) Robotics
5. The ability of a digital copy to be exactly the same as the original is called ______.
 (a) compression (b) convergence (c) lossless duplication (d) programmability

**Answers:** 1(b) 2(b) 3(b) 4(c) 5(c)

### B. Theory Questions
1. Define digital technology. **(2 marks)**
2. State four differences between analogue and digital technology in tabular form. **(8 marks)**
3. Explain five ways digital technology has changed daily life in Nigeria, giving a specific Nigerian example in each case. **(10 marks)**
4. Outline five challenges facing the adoption of digital technology in Nigeria and suggest one solution to each. **(10 marks)**
5. Mention any five careers in digital technology and state one duty of each. **(10 marks)**

### C. Class Activity
In groups of five, students list every digital device they used or came into contact with in the last 24 hours, classify each as input, output or communication, and present findings in two minutes per group.

## TEACHER'S HELPER NOTES — WEEK 1

- **Set the tone for the whole term.** Tell students on day one that Digital Technologies is examined both in theory and in practical work, and that their notebooks and practical files count towards the CA score.
- **The commonest misconception** is that "digital technology" means "computers" or "the internet". Correct it early with the wall-clock/digital-clock demonstration; also point out that a digital wristwatch with no internet is still digital technology.
- **Second misconception:** students equate *electronic* with *digital*. Use the analogue radio counter-example.
- **If you have no computer laboratory**, this lesson still works perfectly. Every student's phone is the teaching aid. Ask them to open the calculator, the camera and the clock, and explain that all three are the same digital circuit running different software — this previews Week 6 (software).
- **Time trap:** the "relevance in Nigeria" discussion is enjoyable and will overrun. Cap it at 15 minutes, put three examples on the board and assign the rest as note-copying homework.
- **Differentiation:** for fast learners, ask why a scanned photograph of a document is bigger in file size than the typed document — this seeds Week 10 (data representation). For slower learners, focus on being able to give three analogue and three digital examples correctly.
- **Language tip:** encourage students to answer in complete sentences. WAEC-style marking rewards explanation, not one-word answers.
- **Cross-curricular link:** Economics (financial inclusion), Civic Education (digital citizenship), Physics (signals and waves).
